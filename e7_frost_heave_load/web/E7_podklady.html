E7 documents – freezing and thawing under load

Unsaturated sand ⌀100 × 100 mm in a 3D-printed cell ⌀160 freezes and thaws in a chest freezer (the same one as in E6). An aluminium frame only guides a dead weight on a partial piston ⌀50, so the load stays constant and the piston moves freely with the surface. Deformation is measured by a camera with a multi-line laser (free annulus around the piston) and an LVDT (piston). Data are logged by a CR1000.

Points to watch

  • Camera: the lens focuses from 0.3 m, but the camera is ≈ 0.17 m from the surface. Refocus with the back-focus ring on the HQ camera (≈ 0.2 mm shift).
  • Laser: the 41° pattern gives a line spacing of ≈ 14 mm from the current position (≈ 134 mm from the centre, 45°), i.e. about 7–8 lines on ⌀100 instead of 11 at 9 mm. A 9 mm spacing needs a narrower DOE (≈ 28°) or a laser closer to the sample. Confirm with Picotronic.
  • Collar shadow: with the laser at 45°, the collar (34 mm above the sand) shadows a ≈ 25 mm strip next to the wall on the laser side, where 2–3 lines are missing. A steeper laser (≈ 65–70°) almost removes the shadow but lowers the sensitivity to ≈ 0.4 × heave.
  • LVDT in the freezer: sources give a lower operating limit of −40 °C or only −10 °C. Confirm operation at −10 °C with RDP or Elkome.
