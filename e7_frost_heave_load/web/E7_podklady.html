E7 documents – freezing and thawing under load

Unsaturated sand ⌀100 × 100 mm in a 3D-printed cell ⌀160 freezes and thaws in a chest freezer (the same one as in E6). An aluminium frame only guides a dead weight on a partial piston ⌀50, so the load stays constant and the piston moves freely with the surface. Deformation is measured by a camera with a multi-line laser (free annulus around the piston) and an LVDT (piston). Data are logged by a CR1000. Revision B follows the measurement review: the cell is insulated so it freezes from the top, and the LVDT and camera reference sit on Invar.

stress under the piston 5–25 kPa (1–5 kg) load travel ±25 mm cycle 24 h −10 °C / 24 h +10 °C point M: TDR CS645 + Pt100 + optical fibre

Points to watch

  • Decide first: at Sr ≈ 0.4 the ice expansion fits into the pore air and the expected heave is tens of µm at most. For frost heave, raise Sr to ≥ 0.85 or supply water from below.
  • Blank run: one full cycle with an Al dummy gives the rig drift against cell temperature; in rev. B the model leaves ≤ 4 µm after the correction.
  • Camera: focus at ≈ 135 mm with the back-focus ring; calibrate px → mm with shims under a flat plate.
  • LVDT in the freezer: sources give a lower operating limit of −40 °C or only −10 °C. Confirm with RDP or Elkome.

Open questions

  1. Target saturation of the sample (see the review).
  2. TDR CS645 rod spacing: 8.9 or 9 mm? How many CS645 probes will we use?
  3. Free height above the sand: 34 or 35 mm? Optical fibre offset: 4.7 or 3 mm?
  4. Does the Pt100 measure at the tip or along its 25 mm length?
  5. Inside dimensions of the freezer: the rig is ≈ 598 mm tall, the estimated inside height is only ≈ 650 mm.
